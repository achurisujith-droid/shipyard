Component library

16 tested pieces a Shipyard project can install instead of asking an agent to invent them. Each one ships with its own tests, and those tests run inside the project it was installed into.

This page is somewhere to look. The app installs from the copy inside the installer, offline — nothing is fetched over the network and written into your project.

Capabilities

Things a product owes its users. A missing one can block a launch.

A way for you to see what is going on

verified

An admin area with a navigation menu that only shows what the person signed in is actually allowed to open.

Use it when you need to an admin area to see what is going on, back office for staff, internal dashboard.

What it does not do
  • Hiding a menu item is not security. The page behind it still has to check the permission itself, which is why the guard is a separate function you have to call.
  • Plain HTML and CSS. No component framework, no charts, no data tables.
admin_dashboard_shell v1.0.0 MIT

A record of who did what

verified

An append-only log of every meaningful action, so you can answer 'who changed this?' months later.

Use it when you need to record who did what and when, a history of changes, see who deleted a record.

What it does not do
  • Append-only is enforced by this component, not by the database. Someone with direct database access can still delete rows.
  • It records what your code tells it. An action that never calls audit() leaves no trace.
audit_logging v1.0.0 MIT

Signing in

verified

Email and password sign-in with sessions people can be signed out of, and the password rules that stop the obvious attacks.

Use it when you need to let people sign in with an email and password, create an account, log in and log out.

What it does not do
  • Email and password only. No Google or Microsoft sign-in, and no two-factor.
  • It does not send a verification email — install the email component if you need one.
  • Password reset is not included.
auth v1.0.0 MIT

Work that happens in the background

verified

Jobs that run after the page has already responded, and retry themselves when they fail.

Use it when you need to do slow work in the background, run a job after the page responds, retry something that failed.

What it does not do
  • Built on your database rather than a dedicated queue. Fine into the thousands of jobs an hour; not a replacement for a real queue at high volume.
  • Jobs run at least once, not exactly once. A job that charges a card must check whether it already did.
  • No scheduled or recurring jobs.
background_job_runner v1.0.0 MIT

A person checking the decisions

verified

When your app makes a decision about someone, a person reviews it before it takes effect — and the reasoning is kept.

Use it when you need to a person checks the decision before it takes effect, approve or reject before it is final, review queue for a human.

What it does not do
  • It makes the review happen and records it. It cannot make the review thoughtful.
  • No appeals process. If someone wants to contest a decision, you need one of those too.
  • It does not decide which of your decisions are consequential enough to need review. That is your judgement.
human_review_workflow v1.0.0 MIT

Keeping customers separate

verified

Each customer's data belongs to their own organisation, and one customer can never read another's.

Use it when you need to each customer only sees their own data, separate one company from another, teams or workspaces.

What it does not do
  • Separation is enforced in the application, not by the database. A query written without scopedWhere() can still read across organisations — which is why the guard exists and why the tests check it.
  • No per-row sharing between organisations.
organization_tenancy v1.0.0 MIT

Database foundation

verified

Somewhere to keep your information, with a health check that tells you when it is not reachable.

Use it when you need to keep information so it is still there tomorrow, save data in a database, store records permanently.

What it does not do
  • The health check tells you the database answered. It does not tell you the data is correct.
  • No backup or restore. That is a separate decision and a separate check.
postgres_schema v1.0.0 MIT

Handing someone their data back, or deleting it

verified

When a person asks for a copy of their data or asks you to erase it, you can do both — and prove you did.

Use it when you need to let someone download all their data, delete a person's data when they ask, gdpr data request.

What it does not do
  • It deletes from your database. Copies in backups, in your email provider, in your analytics or in a spreadsheet on somebody's laptop are not touched.
  • The registry is filled in by you. Nothing can detect a table you forgot to list.
  • Whether your handling satisfies the law where you operate is a question for a lawyer, not this component.
privacy_export_delete v1.0.0 MIT

Who is allowed to do what

verified

Roles and permissions, so an ordinary member cannot do the things only an owner should.

Use it when you need to only admins can do certain things, different permissions for different people, control who is allowed to delete.

What it does not do
  • Three fixed roles. No custom roles you can define at runtime.
  • Permissions apply to the whole organisation. There is no per-record sharing.
rbac v1.0.0 MIT

Letting people upload files

provisional

Uploads that go straight to storage rather than through your app, with limits on what and how big.

Use it when you need to let people upload files, store uploaded documents and images, attach a file to a record.

What it does not do
  • Not run against a live bucket here. The key handling and the limits are tested; the round trip is not.
  • No virus scanning. If strangers can upload, you need that as well.
  • No image resizing or thumbnails.
s3_file_storage v1.0.0 MIT

Knowing when it breaks

provisional

Errors your users hit get reported to you, with enough detail to fix them and no personal data attached.

Use it when you need to find out when the app breaks, get alerted about errors, track crashes in production.

What it does not do
  • Not verified against a live Sentry account here. The scrubbing is tested; the delivery is not. That is what the test event is for.
  • Errors only. No performance tracing, no session replay — both of these record far more about your users.
  • It reports errors your code throws. A page that renders the wrong number without failing is invisible to it.
sentry_error_monitoring v1.0.0 MIT

Taking payment

provisional

Subscriptions through Stripe, with payment messages proved genuine before they are believed and never counted twice.

Use it when you need to take payment from customers, charge a monthly subscription, accept card payments.

What it does not do
  • Not run against a live Stripe account here. The signature check and the double-charge protection are tested with Stripe's own test helpers; the money has never moved.
  • One plan at a time. No usage billing, no metered pricing, no proration handling beyond what Stripe does itself.
  • Tax and invoicing are left to Stripe. Whether that satisfies your local rules is a question for an accountant, not this component.
stripe_subscription_billing v1.0.0 MIT

Emailing your users

verified

Send the emails your app has to send, with a provider you can change and a development mode that never emails a real person.

Use it when you need to send an email to the user, email a receipt or confirmation, welcome email when someone signs up.

What it does not do
  • Sends one email at a time. Not a newsletter tool.
  • No delivery tracking, opens or bounces — those come from the provider's own dashboard.
  • The Resend provider is written against their documented API and has not been run against a live account here.
transactional_email v1.0.0 MIT

Utilities

Jobs of work. A missing one just means somebody writes it by hand.

Importing a spreadsheet

verified

Let somebody upload a CSV, match up their column names to yours, and tell them exactly which rows are wrong.

Use it when you need to let people upload a spreadsheet of data, bulk import records from a csv file, import a list of customers or products.

What it does not do
  • CSV and tab-separated files. Not .xlsx — a spreadsheet has formulas, several sheets and merged cells, and reading one properly is a different job.
  • It checks the shape of each value, not whether it is true. It cannot tell you the email address belongs to a real person.
  • Everything is held in memory, so very large files (hundreds of thousands of rows) need a different approach.
csv_import v1.0.0 MIT

Reading uploaded documents

verified

Pull the text out of a PDF or Word file somebody uploaded, and say plainly when it cannot be done.

Use it when you need to read the text out of an uploaded PDF, pull text from a Word document somebody uploaded, let candidates upload their CV and read it.

What it does not do
  • It reads text that is already text. A scanned page is a picture of words, and this returns nothing for it — it says so rather than returning an empty string.
  • No OCR. If you need to read scans, that is a paid service and a separate decision.
  • It gives you the words, not the meaning. Working out which bit is somebody's phone number is your job.
  • Old .doc files (Word 97) are not supported. Only .docx.
document_text_extract v1.0.0 MIT

Making PDFs

verified

Turn your own data into a PDF somebody can download — an invoice, a receipt, a report.

Use it when you need to generate a pdf invoice, let people download a receipt as a pdf, produce a report as a pdf.

What it does not do
  • Text and tables. No images, no charts, no custom fonts — the built-in fonts cover Latin alphabets only, so this cannot set Greek, Cyrillic, Arabic or CJK text.
  • It does not do the arithmetic for you beyond totalling the lines you give it. What VAT applies is your decision and your accountant's.
  • An invoice produced here is not automatically a legal invoice. What has to appear on one differs by country.
pdf_generate v1.0.0 MIT

What the labels mean

LabelWhat it means
verifiedIts own tests were run against a real install and passed.
provisionalInstalls and its logic is tested, but part of it needs a real account to prove.
experimentalHere to be looked at. Not relied on.

verified means something ran. It is not a judgement about how good the code looks — the contract tests were executed against a real install of the starter template and passed, and nothing else earns it.
